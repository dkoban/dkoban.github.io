\section{Heading Level 1}

All major headings are numbered and set in 11pt bold. The first paragraph after any
section title is indented 0.5 inches with no line space before it.

All subsequent paragraphs are indented the same way with no line spacing between
paragraphs. Here begins the body of your paper and your Introduction section. The body is
typically divided into sections with headings such as Method, Results, and Discussion.

\subsection{Heading Level 2}

The first paragraph after any section title should be indented as here.

The second and all subsequent paragraphs should also be indented (0.5 inches).

\subsubsection{Heading Level 3}

Avoid using more than three levels of headings.

\section{Equations}

Equations are numbered consecutively beginning with (1) to the end of the paper. The number
is enclosed in parentheses and set flush right on the same line as the equation.
\begin{equation}
  \hat{y} = \beta_0 + \sum_{i=1}^{n} \beta_i x_i
\end{equation}

\section{Figures and Tables}

All tables are numbered consecutively and captioned; the caption is centered above the
table as shown in Table~\ref{tab:example}. The body of the table should be no smaller than
9pt.

\section{Citations and References}

Within the text, cite references with the last name of the author(s) and the year of
publication. Use \verb|\textcite{}| for narrative citations, as in \textcite{sage2009},
and \verb|\parencite{}| for parenthetical citations \parencite{blanchard2011}. Add
your sources to \texttt{references.bib}; the reference list below is generated
automatically in APA style with hanging indents.
